    How the model works

    Pacing: across 50 girls’ and women’s swims and 49 boys’ and men’s swims from 45.17 to 1:03.12 and 39.91 to 52.76, the second 50 averaged about 8 to 9 percent slower than the first 50, and the middle half of all swims fell between 7.5 and 10 percent. That share barely changes with level for girls. Faster boys go out a little harder, so the boys’ ideal leans slightly more front-loaded at the top end.

    50-to-100 projection: paired 50 and 100 swims from the same meets put the 100 at roughly 2.15 to 2.20 times the 50 for the fastest swimmers and about 2.22 to 2.26 times for slower high school swimmers.

    Underwaters: elite references include a 2018 analysis of Dressel’s 50 yard record (about 12.9 meters and six kicks off the start, about 11.2 meters and six or seven kicks off the turn) and a 2025 study of elite long-course 100 free turns (men breaking out near 7.1 meters, women near 5.8 meters). High school values are scaled estimates, not measurements.
